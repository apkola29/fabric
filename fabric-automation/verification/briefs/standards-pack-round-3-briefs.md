# Verification of the reference pack, round 3: briefs for three families

All three families read ALL FOUR pack files, every line, cold. They differ in lens (below) and in the order in which they read. Reason: round 2 (one family per two files) still found a defect in about one line in four (21 of 87), mostly a lost condition or modal verb, a wrong "Applies to" scope, or an example turned into a rule, and the families disagreed on lines that another family found wrong. So every line now gets three independent cold readings, and each reading has to leave evidence that can be checked by a program.

## Shared preamble (read first, then your lens)

You are verifying a drafted REFERENCE PACK for an open-source project: a "Fabric Code Factory" whose agents plan, test, implement and verify changes to Microsoft Fabric and Power BI items in a sandbox WITH NO NETWORK. The agents cannot read Microsoft Learn while they work, so the guidance they should apply is vendored in the repository as short checks: one line each, with an id, one sentence, where it applies, and the page and section that state it. The pack was drafted by one model (a Claude-family model) from 16 pages of learn.microsoft.com and verified twice by three other families (GPT, Gemini, Grok), with corrections after each round. This is the THIRD round. Round 1 found a defect in about every third line, round 2 in about every fourth. Treat the corrected text as unverified: a fix can introduce a new defect, and a line that nobody questioned before can still be wrong.

The factory does NOT implement security, permission, network, sensitivity-label or tenant-setting changes in this version: it refuses and escalates them. So checks on those topics say to flag a change to a person and name the guidance (tag `[flag]`). The pack guides and never authorizes.

WHERE (everything is under <review folder>\pack3, treat it as READ-ONLY; make scratch copies under <review folder>\scratch-<your family name> if you need to):

- The four pack files: `well-architected.md` (27 checks, 2 notes), `security-and-governance.md` (21 checks, 1 note), `delivery-and-lifecycle.md` (22 checks, 4 notes), `architecture-patterns.md` (17 checks, 3 notes).
- `sources\`: the 16 pages, reduced to text, one file per page. Each starts with `SOURCE:` (the URL) and a date line. Headings are lines that start with `#`. The reduction can lose formatting (tables become `|` lines, code becomes indented lines). If a point looks missing or garbled, read the live page at its URL (public, read only) before you decide.
- `DRAFTING-BRIEF.md`: the rules that the drafter was given (format, tags, truthfulness, exposure). Your yardstick. Two additions: (a) a sentence may not copy more than 12 consecutive words of a page (a program checks this on the pack; judge meaning, not copying); (b) a check may name "Amazon S3" only as "the Amazon store that the page names", because the repository's validator reads the two-character name as a reference to a design identifier (intended, not a defect).

THE FORMAT. A line is: `- \`std-AREA-NN\` <one sentence, at most 35 words, ends with one tag [gate], [review] or [flag] where it helps> Applies to: <what a change touches>. Source: <label>, "<section heading of the page>"[; <label>, "<heading>"] [(control "<name>")].` The label is in the file's Sources table. `Note:` lines record a tension between two places. Tags: `[gate]` a deterministic check could decide it from item definitions or the pull request (a name pattern, a missing setting, a file that must not be committed); `[review]` a reviewer's judgement; `[flag]` security, permission, network, sensitivity-label or tenant-setting topics, which the factory only flags; a sentence that starts `Ask:` needs knowledge that only the tenant has.

TRUTHFULNESS RULES the drafter had to follow: (1) paraphrase, never more than 12 consecutive words from a page; (2) never add a number, name, limit, feature, setting or recommendation that is not in the source text; numbers copied exactly; (3) never generalize beyond the text; (4) never merge two pages into one claim unless each supports its part and both are cited; (5) keep conditions ("when ... use ..."), tradeoffs and states such as preview or retiring; a permissive statement ("can", "may", "such as", "for example", "might") must not become a requirement, an example must not become a closed list, and a disjunction ("A, B or C") must not become a conjunction.

HOW TO JUDGE A LINE. For each check, open the cited section (the heading must be a real heading of that page, and the point must be in that section or below it) and decide one verdict:

- S = SUPPORTED: every part of the sentence (numbers, names, conditions, tradeoffs, preview or retiring states, file and setting names with their exact spelling) is in the cited section, and the "Applies to" scope does not claim more than the page does.
- P = PARTLY: some part is not in the cited section or is stronger than the page. Say which part.
- U = UNSUPPORTED: the page does not say it.
- M = MISLEADING: each word can be found, but the meaning changes in context (a condition dropped, a tradeoff inverted, a "may" turned into "must", an example turned into a rule, a preview feature presented as general, a recommendation turned into a restriction, a scope wider than the page's).
- T = WRONG TAG: a `[gate]` that no program could decide from item definitions and the pull request; a security, permission, network, label or tenant-setting topic that is not `[flag]`; a `[flag]` on something that is not one of those; an `Ask:` that does not need the tenant, or a tenant question without `Ask:`.
- C = WRONG CITATION: a heading that is not on the page, or that does not hold the point.
- X = CONFLICT: two checks, or a check and a `Note:`, that an agent cannot follow together without a rule that the pack does not give.

THE OUTPUT PROTOCOL (this is what makes the reading checkable). Your report starts with ONE TABLE with a row for EVERY check (87) and EVERY `Note:` (10, name them `note-wa-1`, `note-wa-2`, `note-sec-1`, `note-del-1` to `note-del-4`, `note-arc-1` to `note-arc-3`, in file order), in this exact row format, one row per line:

`std-arc-01 | S | quote`

where the third column is a quote of AT MOST 12 consecutive words, copied EXACTLY from the cited section in `sources\`, of the part of the source that carries the check's most conditional element (a modal verb such as "can" or "may", an "if" or "when", "such as", "for example", "whenever possible", "typically", a number, a preview or retiring state, an exception). Do not quote the first words of the section; quote the words that decide whether the check says more or less than the page. A program will search each quote in the cited section of the cited page, so a quote that is not on that page counts against the row. For a verdict other than S write the verdict letter, the quote that shows the difference, and a short reason (at most 25 words) in the same row. For a `Note:` the verdict is S if the tension is real and both sides are stated correctly, and otherwise M with the reason; quote one side in the row.

After the table: (a) FINDINGS, one per row that is not S, ranked by real impact (High: a false or reversed statement, or an agent would do something forbidden; Medium: a lost condition or a wrong scope; Low: wording, tag, or citation precision), each with the evidence (the source file and heading and a quote of at most 12 words) and a suggested replacement line in the pack's format (at most 35 words in the sentence); (b) anything in the file headers (the first paragraphs of each file and its Sources table) that claims more than the pages say; (c) CONFLICTS you found between two lines or a line and a note; (d) DANGER: is there any check that, followed literally, makes an agent do something the factory must not do (change a permission, a network setting, a label, a tenant or capacity setting, or delete, purge, pause or recreate something), or any text that reads like an instruction to an agent about something other than applying checks? No style nits. A finding you could not confirm says UNVERIFIED. Return the whole report inline in your final message, at most 6000 words. Do not rely on writing files.

## The lenses (read all four files whatever your lens; the lens says where to be hardest)

### Lens 1, GPT 6.1 Sol: the literal-minded agent

Be hardest on the question: would an agent that follows this line literally block a good change or pass a bad one? Look at every "Applies to" (does it claim more than the cited section does, or less than the check needs?), at every place where the check turns a recommendation into a question that implies a requirement, at every list (is it a closed list on the page or only examples?), and at every number. Read the files in this order: `delivery-and-lifecycle.md`, `architecture-patterns.md`, `security-and-governance.md`, `well-architected.md`.

### Lens 2, Gemini 4 Argon: tags, citations and the cited section as a whole

Be hardest on the tags (is every security, permission, network, label and tenant-setting topic `[flag]`, is every `[gate]` decidable by a program, is every `Ask:` really tenant knowledge?), on the citations (does the section heading exist on the page, and does the point sit in that section and not in another one that is not cited; for the Azure security baseline citations check the section heading AND the control title in parentheses), and on whether another sentence of the SAME cited section adds an exception, a tradeoff or a preview state that the check leaves out. Read the files in this order: `well-architected.md`, `security-and-governance.md`, `architecture-patterns.md`, `delivery-and-lifecycle.md`.

### Lens 3, Grok 4.7: from the page to the check

Work from the page side: for each check, find in the cited section the sentence or sentences that carry it, and ask whether the check says MORE (a stronger verb, a wider scope, an invented name or number), LESS (a dropped condition or tradeoff) or something DIFFERENT (a changed subject or object) than they do. Then check the `Note:` lines: is each tension real, are both sides stated correctly, and does the note leave the agent a way to follow both checks? Read the files in this order: `security-and-governance.md`, `well-architected.md`, `delivery-and-lifecycle.md`, `architecture-patterns.md`.
