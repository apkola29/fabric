# Verification of the reference pack: findings, checks and outcomes

The reference pack is the content of the `factory-standards` skill of the kit: Microsoft's published guidance for Fabric workloads (the Well-Architected pages, the Fabric security pages and the Azure security baseline, the Cloud Adoption Framework governance page, the lifecycle (CI/CD) pages and the architecture pages) written as short checks. It is vendored in the repository because the agents of a job have no network. It has four files with 89 checks and 10 notes in all, and every check carries an id, one sentence, what it applies to, and the page and section that state it, with the date of each page at the top of its file. The raw notes on the 16 pages are in [t11-reference-frameworks.md](../research/raw/t11-reference-frameworks.md).

A model of one family (Claude) drafted it from text extracted from the 16 pages, and models of three other families then checked every line against the same text. They did it four times, and each round found more than the author had hoped. This record says what each round was, what it found, what was changed, where the reviewers disagreed and how that was settled, and what is still not proved. The reviewers' replies are kept as they wrote them, with the agent's own header removed.

## Round 1

| Reviewer | Slice | Reply | Findings (as rated by the reviewer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1: GPT 6.1 Sol | `well-architected.md` and `architecture-patterns.md`, every line, and the headers | [standards-pack-round-1-gpt.md](standards-pack-round-1-gpt.md) | 8 checks (1 High: a `[gate]` on a point that no program can decide; 6 Medium: lost conditions and a wrong name; 1 Low), 1 of 4 notes, 1 header |
| P2: Gemini 4 Argon | `security-and-governance.md` and `delivery-and-lifecycle.md`, every line, and the headers | [standards-pack-round-1-gemini.md](standards-pack-round-1-gemini.md) | 14 checks (2 High: tags; 5 Medium; 7 Low) |
| P3: Grok 4.7 | A sample of 24, the points of the pages that the pack leaves out, conflicts, tags, wording that an agent could misread | [standards-pack-round-1-grok.md](standards-pack-round-1-grok.md) | 12 of the 24 sampled checks, 8 omissions (1 High), 7 conflicts (2 High), 3 pieces of wording |

The defects were of six kinds. (1) A condition of the page was lost: "whenever possible", "when the item type supports it", "can" turned into a requirement. (2) The tag was wrong: a `[gate]` on points that no program can decide from an item definition (the retrieval of a secret from a vault, which variable library is in use, a workflow secret), or a `[review]` on something that the factory has to flag. (3) A name was wrong or broader than the page: the storage services that the page names for a shortcut, the file name `.schedules`. (4) Two checks could not both be followed (the medallion page says that bronze allows no changes and also recommends Delta tables for a relational source; the logicalId of an existing item must not change and a copy needs a new one; a layout that one check flags and another asks the agent to design). (5) Wording that reads as an order after "guidance:" ("assign roles", "create and use managed private endpoints") or a purge that a cost check mentions. (6) The headers said more than the pages: the architecture file said that each check keeps the conditions of its page, the Well-Architected file said "implementing" next to security lines, and the security file named five pages and used six.

All 34 defective checks, the three notes and the headers were fixed. The omissions that Grok listed as points that a pull request can decide became new checks: the active value set of a variable library (`std-del-20`), the ETL logic in a post-deploy script (`std-del-21`), the update of an app through the deployment pipelines API (`std-del-22`), a customer-managed key (`std-sec-15`), workspace-level and tenant-level private links (`std-sec-16`), who may create workspaces (`std-sec-17`), the seven-day floor of a vacuum (`std-arc-17`). The Key Vault part of the old `std-sec-01` became `std-sec-14`, and the modular design of the overview became `std-rel-06`. The eighth omission (delete a feature branch after the pull request) was not added: the page calls it a common practice, and it is not a point that a change can get wrong. The `[gate]` tag was kept only where a program can decide the point from the item definitions or the pull request.

## Round 3

All three families read all 97 rows (the 87 checks and the 10 notes of that day), each with a lens: GPT the literal-minded agent (would the line block a good change or pass a bad one?), Gemini the tags, the citations and the cited section as a whole, Grok the path from the page to the line (does it say more, less or something different?). Each row had to carry a verbatim quote of at most 12 words of the cited section, chosen where the check is most conditional. A program looked each quote up in the cited section of the cited page: all 291 were found, and none was in another section or on another page.

|---|---|---|
| Grok 4.7 | [standards-pack-round-3-grok.md](standards-pack-round-3-grok.md) | 16 (15 checks and 1 note): 12 Medium, 4 Low (as rated by the reviewer; the note is one of the four) |

| GPT 6.1 Sol | [standards-pack-round-3-gpt.md](standards-pack-round-3-gpt.md) | 12 checks: 9 Medium, 3 Low (tags) |

Twenty of the 26 checks that were questioned were found by one family only, which is the reason for reading every line three times. Three families found the lost "can" of `std-del-03` and the universal scope of `std-del-01`; two found the closed lists of `std-del-02`, `std-del-17` and `std-del-18`, and `std-del-14`. What was changed: 22 checks, the fourth note of the delivery file, and one check added.

How the reviewers disagreed, and what was decided:

- **`std-del-14`.** Round 2 said that "a rule" is not in the cited sections (and it was removed). Round 3 said that the method list was then closed and left out deployment rules, which the page names in a section that was not cited. The line now says "for example" and the section is cited.
- **`std-gov-07`.** The page adds in the same section that an approved alternative applies when an item cannot use Git integration or deployment pipelines. The line was already at the limit of 35 words, so the exception became its own check (`std-gov-10`) and the header of the delivery file says that the release-pattern checks are `std-gov-05` to `std-gov-07` and `std-gov-10`.
- **`std-arc-02`.** In round 1 Grok found that the check allowed bronze changes that the page forbids and conflicted with `std-arc-04`; the fix was a note. In round 3 Grok found that the check had dropped the page's "no changes are allowed". The check says it again and the note (which says that the page does not say whether a format change is a change) is what lets an agent follow both.
- **`std-del-01`, `std-del-03`.** Three families flagged each. `std-del-01` is now conditional on a feature-workspace project whose item definitions exist and asks the person about the branch policy (`Ask:`), because the policy is a setting of the Git host that no file shows. `std-del-03` is scoped to the Azure Pipelines workflow that the page describes and asks whether the design considers a validation pipeline, as the page says that one "can" run.
- **The tags of `std-sec-01`, `std-sec-03` and `std-del-15`.** GPT read the drafting rule "security topics are `[flag]`" literally and marked three lines whose tags are `[gate]` (a secret in a file; a credential file) and `[review]` (is security validation part of the workflow?). The other two families accepted the tags. The decision: `[flag]` is for a change to security, permissions, a network setting, a sensitivity label or a tenant setting, which the factory refuses; what a change itself contains, such as a secret in a file, is not a change to a setting, and is a `[gate]` when a program can decide it. The headers of the two files that hold such lines and the skill now say so. `std-sec-03` stays `[review]`: it asks a reviewer about the repository's validation workflow and changes nothing.

## Round 4, follow-up

The families that had questioned the two lines checked the rewritten text ([the replies](standards-pack-round-4-followup.md)). Grok found both supported. GPT found `std-sec-14` supported, and found `std-del-10` still too wide: the section says that all the BI creators of a workspace are probably wanted with access to the pipeline, and the rewritten line joined that advice to the repository. The author read the section again, agreed, and split the line. `std-del-10` keeps who may deploy to production, and the new `std-del-23` covers access to the repository or the deployment pipeline: users who take part in content creation, and probably all the BI creators of the workspace for the pipeline. Both cite the same section and both are `[flag]`.

GPT added that it could not find the reports of the other two families and so did not claim a closure on their points, which is why the three lines then went to all three families. They judged `std-sec-14`, `std-del-10` and `std-del-23` in the row protocol of the earlier rounds and were asked to look for a collision with `std-del-19` and with `std-sec-01` and `std-sec-02`. All nine verdicts were supported, no family found a collision, and the program that looks up the quotes found all 13 quotes of the five replies in the cited sections. The three agreed on the tags: `std-sec-14` reviews how code obtains a credential, `std-sec-01` gates a secret in a file, `std-sec-02` flags a static-credential connection, and `std-del-19` is about the identity under which automation calls Fabric APIs, which is a different subject from who may deploy.

## What the author added

1. **Programs that find what a program can find**, run on every round: the structure of every line (the nine tests of `StandardsPackTests`: an id of the form `std-AREA-NN`, one id once, at most 35 words in the sentence, one tag that the skill explains, a label that the table of sources has, only pages of Microsoft Learn); that each cited heading is a heading of the page and each number of a check is in the cited pages; that no run of more than 12 words is copied from a page; that the date in the table of each file is the `updated_at` of the live page (16 of 16); that each quote of a round-3 report is in the cited section; and a table of the hedge words ("can", "may", "such as", "if", "usually", "or") that a source sentence has and the check does not. The author read the table for all 87 checks after round 2 and found nothing that the reviewers had not; round 3 then found 26 checks, so the table is a prompt for a reader and not a substitute for one.
2. **Line-by-line verification of the findings.** Every finding of every round was checked against the extracted text of the page (and the live page where the extraction was in doubt) before the line was changed. Two were not adopted as the reviewer asked: the eighth omission of round 1 (delete a feature branch), which a change cannot get wrong, and the three tags that GPT questioned in round 3, which were kept and explained in the headers. Some others were adopted in a different form from the one that the reviewer suggested, because the limit of 35 words, the rule against copying 12 words, or another reviewer's point ruled it out.
3. **The tests** keep the structure of the pack, and 11 mutations of the pack (an id of another form, a source that no check cites, a tag that the skill does not explain, a sentence over the limit, and so on) are all caught.

## What was not proved

- The pack is a condensation of Microsoft's pages by a model, checked by other models that read the same extracted text. Microsoft did not review it, and it is not a Microsoft checklist or a statement of compliance. A check can still say less than its page (a line that omits a recommendation is not wrong), and a line that three families found supported can still be wrong: every round found lines that the round before had passed, and 20 of the 26 lines that round 3 questioned were questioned by one family alone.
- The rate of defects fell from 44% to 23% and, reading three times, 30% (mostly Low and Medium, none High), and then to 2 of the 24 lines that had been changed. A further round would find some more, and the author stopped because the points had become questions of wording and tags and a person reads the checks as prompts. A check says in its header that it guides and never authorizes.
- That an agent applies the checks well was not tested. The checks were verified as text.
- The pages change without notice. The weekly fact check compares the `updated_at` date of each of the 16 pages with the table of its file and shows a later date as drift; between a change and the refresh by a person, the pack is old.
- The pack does not cover everything that the pages say: it has the points that a change to a Fabric item can get right or wrong and that the pages state as guidance.
