# Verification of the five workload files of the reference pack: findings, checks and outcomes

The reference pack is the content of the `factory-standards` skill of the kit. Its first four files (89 checks, 16 pages; [standards-pack-findings.md](standards-pack-findings.md)) hold the cross-cutting guidance: the Well-Architected pillars, security and governance, lifecycle, architecture patterns. The research notes said, in their list of limits, that Microsoft's own guidance on the workloads themselves was not in the pack ([t11-reference-frameworks.md](../research/raw/t11-reference-frameworks.md)). Five more files now hold it, as short checks with an id, one sentence, where it applies, and the page and section that state it, with the date of each page at the top of the file:

| File | Area | Checks | Notes | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `data-engineering.md` | `eng` | 18 | 10 | 8 (Spark development, monitoring and capacity planning; the native execution engine; Delta layout and V-order; table maintenance; the SQL analytics endpoint; Dataflow Gen2 performance) |
| `semantic-models.md` | `sem` | 18 | 7 | 6 (the two Direct Lake pages; star schema, the optimization guide and data reduction for Import models of Power BI; dimensional modeling for the Fabric Warehouse) |
| `data-agents.md` | `agt` | 16 | 6 | 6 (query-generation practices, configurations, example queries, data sources, semantic models as a source, source control) |
| `capacity-and-cost.md` | `cap` | 16 | 5 | 4 (evaluating and optimizing a capacity, planning a first deployment, growth and governance, throttling and smoothing) |
| `onelake-security.md` | `olk` | 16 | 4 | 4 (best practices, the access control model, table, column and row-level security, the data security overview), and one page of the older pack for one note |

With the older files the pack has 173 checks and 42 notes from 44 pages. A Claude-family model drafted each of the new files from text extracted from the pages (one agent for each file, with the notes of what it read and the SHA-256 of every source file), and models of three other families then checked every line against the same text, in three rounds. This record says what each round was, what it found, what was changed, where the reviewers disagreed and how that was settled, and what is still not proved. The reviewers' replies are kept as they wrote them, with the agent's own header removed.

## Round A

The defects were of six kinds. (1) A tag was wrong: `[review]` on lines whose topic or remedy is a change of a OneLake security role, a data access role, a row-level or column-level rule or a tenant setting, which the factory flags and never makes (four High findings, `std-olk-05`, `std-olk-08`, `std-olk-09`, `std-olk-14`), and a `[gate]` (`std-sem-04`) that joined what a program can read from the model definition (the compatibility level, the mode of the partitions) and the choice of a connector. (2) A condition of the page was lost or turned: "can" became a requirement (`std-sem-17`), a figure that the page gives as an example for two named sizes became a ceiling (`std-cap-15`), a con of several smaller capacities became a con of every move (`std-cap-16`), a rule that the page states for SQL lost its scope (`std-agt-09`), the disjunction "item or data source" was dropped (`std-olk-11`). (3) A name was not the page's: the exact name of a tenant setting (`std-olk-14`), of a Microsoft Entra setting (`std-olk-04`). (4) A citation did not hold the point: the limit of five data sources is on the overview of the data sources page and not on the source control page (`std-agt-01`), and the sentence that source control is a preview is under the title heading (`std-agt-14`). (5) A note claimed a tension that the pages do not state, or an absence (`note-eng-4`, `note-sem-5`, `note-olk-3`), or an example became a general rule (`note-cap-2`, `note-cap-4`, `note-cap-5`). (6) Two lines or two pages could not both be followed: the runtime that the OneLake security page asks for (1.3) and the runtime that the Spark pages call current (2.0); a check on partitioned Direct Lake tables and the older check that asks for Liquid Clustering on new silver and gold tables (`std-sem-08` and `std-arc-07`); the older TLS check and a note of the OneLake file.

The orchestrator gave the fixer five decisions, which it followed. A check whose topic or remedy is a change of a role, a rule or a tenant setting is `[flag]`, in the style of the older pack (`std-sec-06`, `std-sec-11`, `std-sec-12`, `std-sec-13`). A `[gate]` has to be decidable by a program, and a line that is only partly decidable is split, the judgement part becoming a new `[review]` line with the next free number (`std-sem-18`; no id was renumbered). Conflicts with the older pack are resolved in the new file only, by naming the case in the new line and the tension and the older id in a note, without ranking two pages that do not rank each other. A finding that was wrong is rejected with the page text that shows it. The headers of `semantic-models.md` and `onelake-security.md` carry the preview and retiring notices of their pages. The fixer rejected parts of suggestions (all with the page text: a role update that the page does not require, a ranking of the runtimes, a condition "on a Fabric Warehouse" that would turn a recommendation into a condition) and listed what it could not settle, which is repeated under "What was not proved".

## Round C

All three families read the same 7 changed lines, the header and the table of sources of `onelake-security.md`, cold, with a quote of the source for each, and gave a closure row for each of the 7 findings of round B. GPT ([workload-pack-round-c-gpt.md](workload-pack-round-c-gpt.md)) and Gemini ([workload-pack-round-c-gemini.md](workload-pack-round-c-gemini.md)) found all 8 rows supported and all 7 findings fixed, with no new finding. Grok ([workload-pack-round-c-grok.md](workload-pack-round-c-grok.md)) found 7 of the 8 rows supported and 6 of the 7 findings fixed, and one new Low finding: the new clause of `std-olk-12` ended "as std-olk-11 says", and `std-olk-11` says the owner identity only for a SQL analytics endpoint in delegated identity mode, not for Direct Lake over SQL, and the words "delegated SQL analytics endpoints" shortened the condition. The author replaced the clause by the one that Grok suggested ("Direct Lake over SQL, and SQL analytics endpoints whose identity mode is delegated, use the owner identity of the consumer item or data source"), checked the structure of the file with the program and the tests of the pack (0 problems, 9 tests), and did not ask for another reading, because the change is a clause of one `Applies to` and no High or Medium finding remained.

## How the reviewers disagreed and how that was settled

- `note-eng-2`. In round A GPT said that the note claimed a tension that the pages do not state, and the fixer narrowed it. In round B Gemini closed the finding as fixed and Grok found the narrowed note still false in its last clause ("the pages do not give a rule for dividing settings between the environment and `%%configure`"), because "Configuration hierarchy" gives the order of precedence and the advice to standardize at the environment level. The author read the section and the development scenario, agreed with Grok, and changed the clause to the tension that remains: the pages do not reconcile the sparing, debugging use of session overrides with the use of `%%configure` for an interactive and a pipeline phase. All three families supported it in round C.
- `std-olk-09` (a column change on a table that has row-level or column-level rules). Gemini asked for a `[flag]` and for the remedy to be a role update; the fixer made it a flag but did not take the remedy. GPT closed it as a justified rejection (the page states the consequence of a mismatch, not a remedy). The fixer noted that the flag now covers every column change of such a table, although such a change is not itself a security change: the flag rests on the remedy, a rule that only a person updates. The pack follows the convention of the older files.
- `std-sem-08` and the older `std-arc-07`. Grok found that a check on partitioned Direct Lake tables could not be followed together with the older check that asks for Liquid Clustering on new silver and gold tables. The older file was not edited: the new check is conditional on the table being partitioned, names the older id in its `Applies to`, and a note records that the Direct Lake page never mentions Liquid Clustering. Gemini closed it as fixed.
- The runtime. The OneLake security page asks for a Spark environment of 3.5 or higher and runtime 1.3, and the two Spark pages call runtime 2.0 current. No page ranks the other, so `std-olk-16` and `std-eng-01` name each other and rank neither.
- Tags. The fixer followed decision 1 where Grok had suggested `[review]` (`std-olk-08`, `std-olk-14`); GPT closed both as fixed.

## What the author added

- The five files, their five headers, the row for `data-agents.md` in the skill (`SKILL.md`), and the claims that watch the 28 pages (each page has a claim `page-updated-*` that shows a changed `updated_at` as drift; the test of the pack refuses a page that no claim watches). The test of the pack read four files and reads nine now, and its areas are the nine.
- The program that looks up the quotes ignores a line that starts with `#` inside a fenced code block of a page (a shell comment is not a heading); round A had found that it cut a section at such a line.
- One of the older pages (the Patterns page of the architecture file) was republished on 2026-11-02, after the first four files were checked. The upstream change is one reworded sentence. The check lines were read again against the new text, and only the date in the sources table and in the claim moved ([t11-reference-frameworks.md](../research/raw/t11-reference-frameworks.md), section 3).
- The research note says now which workload areas are in the pack (Spark and Delta tables, semantic models, capacity and cost, OneLake security, data agents) and which are not (Eventhouse and Real-Time Intelligence, ontology, pipelines, Warehouse tuning beyond dimensional modeling).

## What was not proved

- The checks are a condensation of the pages by a model, read by other models against the same extracted text. No person who knows Fabric has reviewed them, and no agent has been run with them. A check is a prompt for a reviewer, and the pages themselves say that they give guidance and not a rule.
- `std-sem-04` is a gate on the compatibility level and the mode of the Direct Lake partitions. The page speaks of the metadata that one sees through the XMLA endpoint and of the TMDL view, not of the files in Git, so that a program can read both from the model definition of an item is the fixer's reading (UNVERIFIED).
- Whether the native execution engine is still in preview is not settled (`note-eng-4`): the page says "during the preview phase" and that the mechanisms for the tenant, workspace and environment levels are under active development, and it carries the same update date and wording as when it was read.
- `std-sem-10` records a difference without ranking: the page recommends a dimensional model in a Fabric Warehouse for enterprise solutions and never mentions a lakehouse, so whether it excludes a lakehouse design is not settled.
- `note-olk-4` sets two pages beside each other (the data security page says that outbound communication might fall back to TLS 1.0, and the Fundamentals page says that it now requires TLS 1.2 or later); the note does not rank them, and the older `std-sec-11` still asks the question.
- The correction of `std-olk-12` after round C was not read by another family.
- The pages change without notice. The weekly fact check compares the `updated_at` date of each page; between a change and the refresh by a person the pack is old. The page "Develop Direct Lake semantic models" carried the date of the day on which the files were checked (2026-11-04), and a later date will show as drift.
- Industry guidance from outside Microsoft (and community advice on Spark tuning, modelling and DAX) is not in the pack.
