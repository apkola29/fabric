# Capacity and cost checks for Fabric changes

Checks drawn from four Fabric pages on evaluating and optimizing a capacity, planning a first deployment, managing growth and governance, and throttling and smoothing, for use when a change adds load to a capacity, schedules work or touches a capacity setting. This file guides and never authorizes a change.
The factory does not make a capacity change (its size, pausing it, surge protection, overage billing, moving a workspace to another capacity); it flags the change to a person, so those checks say to flag the change and name the guidance. Buying capacity, reservations, chargeback and organizational roles are not decisions for a pull request and are left out. The throttling page says that Microsoft might change or update the throttling policy, so read its points as of the date in the table.
Tags: [gate] a deterministic check could verify it; [review] needs a reviewer's judgement; [flag] a change to security, permissions, a network setting, a sensitivity label or a tenant or capacity setting, which the factory flags, naming the guidance, and never makes. A check that starts with Ask: needs knowledge of the tenant. A question about pausing or resizing a capacity is a question to ask about the change, never an instruction to do it.

## Diagnosing a capacity problem and sizing a change

- `std-cap-01` If slowness is blamed on throttling, is that confirmed, for example in the capacity metrics app, given that slow performance is often item design and utilization above 100 percent does not automatically mean throttling? [review] Applies to: a change or description that blames slowness on capacity throttling. Source: Throttling, "How to tell that capacity throttling is occurring"; Throttling, "Track overages and rejected operations".
- `std-cap-02` Where a plan adds capacity to fix slowness or throttling, does it show that the workload was optimized first, as the growth page says to optimize before scaling and to expand only when necessary? [review] Applies to: a plan or request that adds or enlarges a capacity. Source: Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)".
- `std-cap-03` Ask: for a new workload on a capacity that already hosts other workloads, do the capacity metrics show how near its limit that capacity runs and whether throttling, delays or rejections occur, which ideally stay at zero? [review] Applies to: a new or heavier workload assigned to a capacity that already hosts workloads. Source: Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)".
- `std-cap-04` Ask: does the capacity SKU that will host a new workload meet the workload's thresholds, which depend on SKU size and license type, for example the capacity requirements of a semantic model? [review] Applies to: a new workload, such as a semantic model, and the capacity that hosts it. Source: Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)".

Note: the growth page says to always optimize workloads before increasing capacity, the optimize page names optimize, scale up and scale out as three strategies for a capacity that shows throttling or rejection without ranking them, and the throttling page says that consistently high throttling points to load balancing across capacities or to a larger size; the pages do not say how much optimization should come first. Source: Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)"; Optimize capacity, "Manage high compute usage"; Throttling, "Right-sizing and optimizing a capacity".

Note: the optimize and planning pages advise starting small and growing a capacity gradually, while the growth page says that each workload has thresholds set by SKU size and license type and that a semantic model which demands a given size should begin on that size; the pages do not say how to reconcile the two. Source: Optimize capacity, "Planning and budgeting tools"; Plan deployment, "Guidance for Microsoft Fabric capacity planning"; Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)".

Note: the pages give no single utilization threshold: for a shared self-service capacity the growth page suggests considering a split of workloads or a larger size when peak use is consistently above about 80 percent, and in both scenarios a scale-up before full utilization when peaks rise steadily; the planning page says to pick a size where the highest pilot usage sits comfortably under 100 percent; and the throttling page says that utilization above 100 percent does not automatically mean throttling. Source: Manage growth, "Self-service (decentralized) capacities"; Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)"; Plan deployment, "Key actions in phase 2"; Throttling, "Track overages and rejected operations".

## How a change draws on a capacity

- `std-cap-05` If a change has one capacity consume items another capacity produces, for example OneLake items, does the plan account for the consuming capacity's throttling state, which decides whether calls to the item are throttled? [review] Applies to: a report, semantic model or other item that reads items produced on another capacity. Source: Throttling, "Understand the Fabric capacity throttling policy".
- `std-cap-06` If a change adopts a preview capability, does the plan size the capacity for when it becomes billable, since some preview capabilities can generate non-billable operations that throttling calculations exclude? [review] Applies to: a change that uses a preview capability. Source: Throttling, "Billable and non-billable compute in throttling calculations".
- `std-cap-07` Ask: does the capacity for a new Spark workload have Autoscale Billing for Spark enabled, in which case Spark usage is pay-as-you-go and bursting and smoothing do not apply? [review] Applies to: a new Spark workload. Source: Throttling, "Smoothing: Spread CU usage across future timepoints".

## Compute use by experience

- `std-cap-08` Does a Spark job or notebook reserve the executors it requires, which frees resources for other Spark jobs and lowers the chance of failures with HTTP 430, meaning too many requests for the capacity? [review] Applies to: a Spark job or notebook. Source: Optimize capacity, "Fabric Data Engineering and Fabric Data Science".
- `std-cap-09` Does a notebook or Spark job definition stop its Spark session when not in use, since active sessions can accrue CU usage and the default expiration time is 20 minutes, which users can change? [review] Applies to: a notebook or Spark job definition. Source: Optimize capacity, "Fabric Data Engineering and Fabric Data Science".
- `std-cap-10` Does a dataflow or pipeline avoid refreshing data more often than its source requires, for example hourly for a source that is only updated once every 24 hours? [review] Applies to: a schedule or trigger of a dataflow or pipeline. Source: Optimize capacity, "Data Factory".
- `std-cap-11` Do new or changed Fabric data warehouse tables use the smallest data types possible, for example a shorter VARCHAR length, since the choice can heavily influence query plans and resources allocated to a query? [review] Applies to: a table definition in a Fabric data warehouse. Source: Optimize capacity, "Fabric Data Warehouse".

Note: the growth page lists rescheduling consecutive pipelines to avoid resource peaks among its steps to optimize before scaling, while the planning page says there is no need to schedule one job after another, and the throttling page says that scheduled jobs can all run simultaneously without spikes that would block jobs from starting; the pages do not say how these fit together. Source: Manage growth, "Common best practices (both scenarios)"; Plan deployment, "Key actions in phase 3"; Throttling, "Smoothing: Spread CU usage across future timepoints".

Note: the optimize page says that all data warehouse operations are background operations, the throttling page says that Fabric reports almost all Warehouse operations as background and can throttle a background operation in a chain started by an interactive operation as interactive, and the growth page says that many interactive operations, such as a SQL query, are also considered background. Source: Optimize capacity, "Fabric Data Warehouse"; Throttling, "Throttling behavior is specific to Fabric workloads"; Manage growth, "Enterprise (centralized) capacities".

## Capacity settings that the factory flags

- `std-cap-12` Flag a change to surge protection on a capacity; guidance: while active it rejects background jobs, which can be delayed or need reruns, and it does not replace optimization, scaling or isolating critical solutions. [flag] Applies to: a surge protection setting of a capacity. Source: Optimize capacity, "Configure surge protection"; Manage growth, "Enterprise (centralized) capacities".
- `std-cap-13` Flag a change that pauses a capacity or turns on overage billing; guidance: pausing results in a billing event and can make content unavailable, and overage billing costs three times the normal rate. [flag] Applies to: pausing, or overage billing, on an F SKU capacity. Source: Throttling, "How to stop throttling when it occurs".
- `std-cap-14` Flag a change that resizes an F SKU capacity, including a scheduled one; guidance: frequent resizing might delay or otherwise affect operations in progress, and resizes should be planned for periods of low activity. [flag] Applies to: a resize of an F SKU capacity, including automation that schedules it. Source: Manage growth, "Enterprise (centralized) capacities".
- `std-cap-15` Flag a capacity downsize; guidance: if large jobs have run, smoothed usage should be well below the threshold, for example under 40 percent at the larger size to be 80 percent at the lower size. [flag] Applies to: a change that moves a capacity to a lower size. Source: Manage growth, "Enterprise (centralized) capacities".
- `std-cap-16` Flag a change that moves workspaces or items to another capacity; guidance: scaling out helps isolate compute for high-priority items, with cost and a higher chance of throttling for high CU items as downsides. [flag] Applies to: a workspace-to-capacity assignment. Source: Optimize capacity, "Scale out"; Plan deployment, "Key actions in phase 3".
