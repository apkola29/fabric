Platform app sample architecture

All in one: a customer runs its own Fabric

The CRM's operational data, the data platform, and the reports and data agents all live in the customer's own Microsoft Fabric, and the customer manages them. Contoso is the example, from its data sources to the people who use the reports.

From data sources to reports and data agents

This is a design for discussion. The sample on GitHub builds an isolated pilot instead, in which the platform runs one workspace per customer in its own Entra tenant. Here the customer manages the environment, and the data flows in five steps:

  • Ingestion. Mirroring keeps a near-real-time copy of databases such as Azure SQL Database and SQL Server. Data Factory pipelines, copy jobs and Dataflow Gen2 load other sources, such as SaaS apps and REST APIs. Shortcuts read files in place, in SharePoint, OneDrive, Azure Data Lake Storage or Amazon S3. Sources on Contoso's network go through an on-premises data gateway.
  • Storage layer. OneLake stores the data once: the CRM's operational data (a SQL database in Fabric, or mirrored databases), a warehouse, and a lakehouse that follows a medallion architecture (bronze, silver and gold tables), all as Delta tables.
  • Transform. Notebooks (Spark) and stored procedures (T-SQL) clean and join the data into business tables.
  • Model. A semantic model reads the business tables with Direct Lake and applies row-level security.
  • Consume / visualize. Reports and data agents serve Contoso's people in Power BI, Teams or Copilot, or inside the CRM app.

Compared with the isolated pilot, Contoso owns the tenant, the capacity, the workspace, the gateway and the connections, and its IT runs them. The platform app, if Contoso uses it, is just the CRM app.
